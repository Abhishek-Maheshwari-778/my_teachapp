Adding live video conferencing (like Google Meet or Zoom) to your TutorFlow SecureNotes app while keeping it cost-effective and low-overhead is entirely possible!

Since you want to avoid expensive paid subscription servers or heavy backend infrastructure, you can leverage free-tier or open-source real-time communication tools that integrate smoothly into Flutter.

How to Implement Live Lectures (Low-Cost Options)
Option 1: Jitsi Meet SDK (Recommended & Free/Open-Source)
What it is: Jitsi is a fully open-source video conferencing platform. They provide free public servers (via meet.jit.si) and a Flutter package (jitsi_meet_flutter_sdk) that lets you embed a complete video meeting room directly inside your app.

Why it fits your app:

Zero Cost: You don’t need to pay for a signaling or media server if you use public Jitsi instances (or you can host your own free server later on a cheap VPS if needed).

Flutter Integration: It has drop-in widgets and plugins that allow students to join a live video class with a single tap from your app's UI.

Security Control: You can set custom room names mapped via your Google Sheet (e.g., only logged-in students with matching class access can see the room link).

Option 2: Embedded Google Meet / Zoom Links
What it is: You create a Google Meet or Zoom link manually for your class, and store that URL in your Google Sheet backend under the live lecture section.

Why it fits your app:

Zero Development Complexity: Your app simply opens the WebView or launches an intent to join the meeting URL.

No Server Overhead: Google or Zoom handles all the streaming, bandwidth, and scaling for thousands of users effortlessly.

Enhancing Security for Live Lectures
To maintain your privacy and anti-piracy goals for live classes:

In-App Webview / Intent Launch: Force students to open the live lecture through your app interface rather than sharing raw public links.

FLAG_SECURE Protection: Just like your notes and video player, ensure the live lecture screen enforces FLAG_SECURE so students cannot record the live session using Android's built-in screen recorders.

Google Sheet Access Control: In your Google Sheets backend, you can add a LiveClass tab where you control who has access. When a student's payment or subscription is verified as true, the app reveals the active meeting button.
